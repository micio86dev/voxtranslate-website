## 2. Everything is a draft, deliberately

Every guide carries `publishedAt: null`; every persona entry in `PERSONAS` does the same.
Consequently all 35 pages render `noindex` and appear in **zero** sitemaps — verified against
the built output: `sitemap-guides.xml` has 0 URLs, `sitemap-pages.xml` is unchanged at 107.

The exit gate asks for pages *live*. Publishing 35 pages of public marketing copy written in
someone else's name, none of which they have read, is not a build step — it is a decision. The
task 00 machinery makes it a one-field change per page when they want it.

## 3. How the content was constrained

**Competitor claims come only from `docs/positioning.md`** (AGENTS.md R1). Every reference to
Zoom's captions-only limit, Teams Interpreter's Microsoft 365 Copilot prerequisite and guest
exclusion, and Google Meet's one-pair-per-meeting cap traces to that file, which carries its own
primary sources and verification date.

**Webex and YouTube are the interesting cases.** Neither appears in `positioning.md`, so the
guides covering them state **no capability figures at all**. The Webex guide says so explicitly
and redirects the reader to their own admin console, then gives the four questions that decide
fitness regardless of platform. Writing a plausible language count there would have been easy
and would have violated R1 exactly as much as inventing one for Zoom.

**No product numbers.** No latency figures anywhere (R2 — those belong to task 01), and the
commercial claims are limited to the three contractual promises `positioning.md` states can be
made: published per-minute pricing, non-expiring credits, no per-language tariff.

**Honest platform advice throughout.** Per the task's tone instruction, each guide says when the
native platform is sufficient — "two languages, one of them English, under 90 minutes, on Meet,
use what you already pay for". Each persona page carries a "when you should not choose us"
section naming the cases where a human interpreter or an established vendor is the right answer.
