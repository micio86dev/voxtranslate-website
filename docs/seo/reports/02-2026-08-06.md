## 2. The twelfth page was not written, and that is the finding

`/alternatives/interactio/` is **deliberately absent**.

`docs/positioning.md` mentions Interactio exactly once — "Interprefy (Swiss) and Interactio (Lithuanian) are the only credible European options, and both are expensive and sales-led" — and **Interactio does not appear in its primary sources list at all**. Every other vendor in batch 1 has at least one linked source.

R1 permits a claim only if it comes from `positioning.md` **or** from a primary source linked on the page with a verification date. One unsourced sentence supports neither a feature table nor a pricing section, and `comparisonSchema` would have forced me to list a `primarySources` URL I had not actually verified.

Writing the page was entirely possible. It would have looked exactly like the other ten, which is the problem: a fabricated comparison is indistinguishable from a real one at review time. Skipping it and saying so is the only version of this that a reviewer can act on.

**To ship it:** someone verifies Interactio's current pricing and language coverage against primary sources, adds them to `positioning.md` with a verification date, and then the page can be written from that.

## 3. How the claims were constrained

**Every factual claim traces to `positioning.md`**, which carries its own verification date (research conducted 5 August 2026) and primary source list. Each page renders that date at the top and links its sources at the bottom — both enforced by `comparisonSchema`, not by discipline.

**The absence of a public price is treated as the story**, per the task. Wordly, Interprefy, KUDO and Boostlingo all decline to publish prices; each page states the structure that *is* documented (Wordly's 10/25/50/100/250/500+ tiers with a 10-hour minimum and 12-month expiry; Interprefy's per-language component and no rollover; KUDO's 50-hour annual floor) and links the vendor's own page.

**Every page opens with what the competitor does well**, before any comparison. Several end up recommending the competitor outright — the JotMe page states plainly that below three hours a month JotMe is cheaper and should be used; the Boostlingo page says that if your requirement names human interpreters the comparison is already decided.

**`whenNotToChooseUs` is substantive on all eleven.** The schema enforces 200 characters; every page runs well past it, and each names specific situations rather than gesturing at humility. The Wordly and voxtranslate-vs-wordly pages both state that we hold none of SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 or HIPAA and that this ends the conversation for regulated buyers.

**No product numbers.** No latency figures (R2 — task 01's territory). Commercial claims limited to the three `positioning.md` says can be made.

## 7. Gotcha worth recording

**Unquoted ISO dates in YAML frontmatter parse as `Date`, not `string`.** `verifiedAt: 2026-08-05T00:00:00.000Z` fails `z.string().datetime()` with "Expected type `string`, received `date`". Quote them. Same class of failure as the unquoted `shortAnswer` containing `: ` in task 03 — both caught by the build, both invisible on inspection.
