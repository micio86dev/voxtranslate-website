## What this is actually good for

Following a stream in a language you do not speak, when the content is worth the effort:
a tournament caster, a developer stream, an interview, a talk being restreamed. Anything
where someone is speaking and you want to know what they are saying.

The subtitles sit over the player and stay there in theatre and fullscreen mode, so you
are not choosing between the stream and the translation.

## Why the original audio control matters more here

On a webinar you might happily mute the original. On a stream, the original audio is a
large part of why you are there — the crowd, the game, the streamer's delivery and timing.

So the mix is a dial rather than a switch. Set the original wherever you like from 0 to
100%; while the translated voice speaks it drops underneath and comes straight back when
it stops. Many people run the original loud and use subtitles only.

## Be realistic about accuracy

Streams are the worst case for speech recognition, and pretending otherwise would waste
your money. Game audio over voice, two people talking at once, community-specific slang,
shouting — all of it degrades transcription before translation even starts, and the
translation cannot recover what was never heard correctly.

It works well for someone speaking clearly into a decent microphone. It works badly for a
chaotic four-person party stream, and we would rather say so here than have you find out
after the fact.
