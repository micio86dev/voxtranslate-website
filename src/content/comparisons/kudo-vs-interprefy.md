## The one-line difference

Both are hybrid — AI plus human interpreters — but the balance differs. **KUDO's centre of gravity is its interpreter marketplace. Interprefy's is its AI platform, with humans available.**

## Side by side

| | KUDO | Interprefy |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Hybrid, interpreter marketplace | Hybrid, AI-first with human escalation |
| Human interpreters | ~12,000, 200+ languages | Yes, live AI→human failover |
| Notice for a human | ~2 hours | — |
| AI languages | 70+ | 82 full, ~19 partial |
| Pricing published | No | No |
| Pricing model | Annual, from 50 hours | Hours **and** number of languages |
| Compliance | SOC 2 Type 1 and 2 | — |
| Jurisdiction | US | Swiss |

## Where each one is stronger

**KUDO** wins on interpreter access. 12,000 interpreters across 200+ languages, bookable at roughly two hours' notice, is a genuine operational capability — it means a session that unexpectedly needs a human can have one the same day. For organisations whose translation need is fundamentally interpretation with software attached, this is the stronger product.

**Interprefy** wins on AI breadth and on the live escalation path. 82 fully supported languages is wider AI coverage, and the ability to move from AI to human **inside a running session** is a different capability from booking an interpreter for the next one. Its Swiss base is a real procurement advantage in Europe.

## The commercial difference

**KUDO sells annual packages from 50 hours.** That is roughly an hour a week — a meaningful floor. If your genuine need is a monthly session, you are buying a package sized for a different organisation.

**Interprefy prices by hours and by language count**, with hours that do not roll over.

Neither publishes a price, so both require a sales conversation before you have a comparable number.

The floor is the sharper distinction. KUDO's 50-hour minimum is a stated threshold; Interprefy's is set in the conversation. If your volume is genuinely low, that difference is worth probing early rather than after two rounds of calls.

## Questions that separate them

1. **KUDO:** what is the real minimum in hours and months, and what happens to unused hours?
2. **KUDO:** what does an on-demand human interpreter cost against a scheduled one?
3. **Interprefy:** exactly how is a "language" counted for billing — per event, per session, per active pair?
4. **Interprefy:** what does escalating to a human mid-session cost, and how quickly does it happen in practice?
5. **Both:** is there a per-session minimum?

## Which to shortlist

- **Human interpretation is central** → KUDO
- **AI is central, humans occasionally** → Interprefy
- **Rare languages needed** → KUDO's 200+ marketplace
- **Wide AI language coverage** → Interprefy
- **European counterparty required** → Interprefy
- **SOC 2 required today** → KUDO
- **Low volume** → probe KUDO's 50-hour floor first; it may rule them out on its own
