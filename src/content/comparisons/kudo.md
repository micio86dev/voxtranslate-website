## What KUDO does well

KUDO's real asset is not its AI — it is a **marketplace of around 12,000 professional interpreters covering more than 200 languages**, with interpreters bookable at short notice.

That is a genuine capability with no software substitute. When a session needs a human who will resolve ambiguity, read the room and take responsibility for the rendering, KUDO can put one there.

It also runs an AI speech translator covering **70+ languages**, and holds **SOC 2 Type 1 and Type 2**.

## Where the fit breaks

KUDO is built around scheduled, consequential sessions — the kind you plan for, book people for, and budget for. Its pricing reflects that: **annual packages starting from 50 hours**, sold through a sales conversation. [KUDO does not publish prices.](https://kudo.ai/pricing)

A 50-hour annual minimum is roughly one hour a week. If your need is a fortnightly supplier call and a couple of standups, you are buying a package sized for a different organisation.

There is also a shape mismatch. Interpreted sessions are structured: someone speaks, others listen, turns are managed. A four-person call where everyone interrupts everyone is not a format human interpretation handles gracefully, and it is not what the platform is designed around.

## The honest framing

These are not substitutes for each other. KUDO sells access to professional interpreters with software around it. We sell software for conversations that will never have an interpreter in them, because nobody books one for a Tuesday check-in.

If you are choosing between us, the question is not which is better — it is which describes the meetings you actually have. If some of your meetings are each kind, using both is a perfectly reasonable answer.
