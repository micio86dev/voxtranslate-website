## Two different products with overlapping vocabulary

Both do AI translation for meetings. That is where the similarity ends, and the difference is architectural rather than a matter of features.

**Wordly is built for broadcast** — a small number of speakers, a large number of listeners, each selecting a language. **We are built for symmetric conversation** — up to four people, each speaking a different language, all being heard.

Those are different technical problems. Broadcast means routing one stream to many listeners. Symmetric conversation means recognising, translating and routing every voice to three different destinations at once, with people interrupting each other.

## Where Wordly is clearly ahead

**Compliance.** SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and HIPAA. We have none of them. For regulated industries and most enterprise procurement, this ends the comparison, and we would rather say so than spend six months in a questionnaire we cannot pass.

**Scale and reference.** 5,000+ organisations. "Who else uses you" is a fair question and their answer is better.

**Language breadth, documented.** 60 speaker languages across 3,000+ pairs is wide coverage from a vendor that documents it — which is more than most of this market does.

## Where we are ahead

**The meeting shape.** Four people in four languages all speaking is not something a broadcast architecture handles. This is the segment, not a limitation we are dressing up.

**Price you can see.** Published per minute. No sales call to find out what it costs.

**Credits that do not expire.** Against a 10-hour minimum with 12-month expiry, this matters specifically when usage is irregular.

**No per-language tariff.** A four-language call is not four times a one-language call.

## The 4-participant cap, honestly

We cap at four active participants. That is a real constraint and it is the definition of the segment rather than a temporary limit.

If you need twelve people actively speaking, we are the wrong product and you should not try to work around it. If what you have is the recurring three-person international call that currently happens in approximate English, that is exactly what this is for.

## How to choose

- **Running events?** Wordly.
- **Need certifications today?** Wordly.
- **More than four active speakers?** Wordly.
- **High, predictable volume?** Wordly will likely beat us on rate.
- **Small group, several languages, all speaking?** Us.
- **Irregular usage?** Us — expiry is a real cost.
- **Need to start this week?** Us.

If you are running both kinds of session, using both products is a perfectly sensible answer, and cheaper than forcing either one to do the other's job.
