## What Wordly does well

Wordly is the strongest pure-AI vendor in the event translation market, and the closest to us philosophically: no interpreter marketplace, no human-in-the-loop, just AI translation sold as a product.

The numbers behind that are real. **60 speaker languages and 7 attendee languages across more than 3,000 pairs.** More than 5,000 customer organisations. **SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and HIPAA** — a compliance position that clears enterprise procurement without an argument.

For a congress, a conference or a large webinar, it does exactly what it says.

## Where the fit breaks

Wordly is architected for **one-to-many broadcast**: a small number of speakers, a large number of passive listeners, each selecting a language. That is a different technical problem from four people in four languages all speaking and all being heard.

If your actual need is the recurring three-person sales call, the daily standup across two offices, or the supplier escalation where the production manager has to be in the conversation, you are buying event infrastructure to solve a conversation problem.

## Pricing

**Wordly does not publish prices.** Its [pricing page](https://www.wordly.ai/pricing) lists annual hour tiers — 10, 25, 50, 100, 250 and 500+ hours — with a **minimum purchase of 10 hours** and a **12-month expiry** on unused hours.

That structure is worth understanding rather than complaining about. If you run predictable volume, buying hours in bulk is rational and the effective rate improves with tier. If your usage is irregular — three heavy months and five quiet ones — you are pre-paying for capacity you may not use, and it disappears at twelve months.

The absence of a public price is the more important fact. You cannot compare Wordly to anything without a sales conversation, which sets a floor on how quickly you can make a decision.

## The honest summary

Wordly is right for its market. If you are running events, talk to them. If you are running conversations, the shape of the product matters more than the feature list, and that is where we differ.
