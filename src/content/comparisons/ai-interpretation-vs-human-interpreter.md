## The question behind the question

Most people asking this are not choosing between two options for one meeting. They are trying to justify a decision internally, and the honest answer changes depending on which meeting you mean.

For a given session, the two are not close substitutes. Across a year of meetings, most organisations need both.

## What a human interpreter does that AI does not

**Resolves ambiguity.** A phrase that could go two ways gets read correctly from context — or the interpreter asks. An algorithm picks the more probable rendering and moves on, with no signal that a choice was made.

**Reads the room.** Hesitation, reluctance, a shift in register. In a negotiation these carry as much information as the words.

**Takes responsibility.** A professional interpreter is accountable for the rendering. That accountability is a large part of what you are buying in a legal or medical setting, and software cannot provide it at any quality level.

**Covers the long tail.** Boostlingo offers 275+ languages through interpreters, KUDO's marketplace 200+. No AI product is close for rare and low-resource languages, and that gap is closing slowly.

## What AI does that a human interpreter does not

**Exists on Tuesday for a Thursday meeting.** Human interpretation typically needs two weeks' notice — KUDO's marketplace is the fast exception at around two hours. Most meetings are arranged inside that window.

**Costs nothing to have available.** Interpreters are priced per language pair per session, with two per pair for anything over 30 minutes and a session minimum. Three languages is three pairs is six people. There is a floor below which the option does not exist.

**Scales across languages without multiplying.** Four languages in a four-person call is four interpreter pairs, or one AI session.

**Serves the meetings nobody would ever book for.** The weekly standup, the supplier check-in, the support escalation.

## The comparison that actually matters

The usual framing is *AI translation versus a human interpreter*. For most of the meetings in a company's week, the real comparison is **AI translation versus nothing** — because nobody was ever going to book an interpreter for a fifteen-minute call, and those meetings currently happen in approximate English with everyone pretending it is fine.

That is a much easier bar, and it is the honest one.

## How to decide, per meeting

| Situation | Choose |
|---|---|
| Legal, regulatory, medical, contractual | Human interpreter |
| Adversarial negotiation | Human interpreter |
| External and reputational — a keynote in their language | Human interpreter |
| Rare or low-resource language | Human interpreter |
| Announced this week | AI |
| Internal, recurring, routine | AI |
| Three or more languages, small group | AI |
| People read the language but not at speaking pace | Neither — captions |
| Everyone genuinely shares a working language | Neither — ask them first |

## What to do with a mixed calendar

Most organisations land on: interpreters for the handful of sessions that matter most, AI for the weekly volume, and nothing for the meetings where translation was never the problem.

If a vendor tells you their AI removes the need for interpreters, treat that as a claim about their sales target rather than about your risk.
