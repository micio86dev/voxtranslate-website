## The one-line difference

**Wordly is pure AI. Interprefy is hybrid** — AI with the ability to bring in human interpreters, including escalation within a session.

Almost everything else follows from that.

## Side by side

| | Wordly | Interprefy |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Pure AI | Hybrid: AI + human interpreters |
| Human escalation | No | Yes |
| Languages | 60 speaker / 7 attendee, 3,000+ pairs | 82 full, ~19 partial |
| Pricing published | No | No |
| Pricing model | Annual hour tiers (10/25/50/100/250/500+) | Hours **and** number of languages |
| Minimum | 10 hours | Via sales |
| Unused hours | Expire after 12 months | Do not roll over |
| Jurisdiction | US | Swiss |
| Compliance | SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, HIPAA | — |
| Scale | 5,000+ organisations | — |

## Where each one is stronger

**Wordly** is the stronger choice when the work is pure AI translation at scale and procurement is the hard part. The compliance position — SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and HIPAA together — clears enterprise review without argument, and 5,000+ organisations is a reference base that answers the "who else uses you" question on its own.

**Interprefy** is stronger when you may need a human. That optionality is real: a session that starts on AI and escalates when the stakes rise is something Wordly structurally cannot offer. Its 82 full languages also exceed Wordly's speaker coverage, and the Swiss base matters in European procurement where counterparty jurisdiction is assessed.

## The pricing difference that decides most cases

Both hide their prices, so neither wins on transparency. The structural difference is what to model.

**Wordly** prices by hours, in tiers, with a 10-hour minimum and 12-month expiry.

**Interprefy** prices by hours **and by number of languages**.

If your events are one or two languages each, Interprefy's language component barely registers and the comparison comes down to rate and capability. If your sessions routinely run four or five languages, that component compounds and Wordly's flat-by-hours model is likely cheaper for the same volume.

Ask both for a quote against **your actual language count per session**, not against total hours. That is the number that separates them.

## The questions to put to both

1. Effective per-hour rate at your realistic tier
2. What counts as an hour — session length, per attendee, per language?
3. Written terms on unused hours
4. Per-session minimum — does a 30-minute meeting bill as an hour?
5. For Interprefy: exactly how a "language" is counted
6. For Wordly: what happens at renewal if usage came in under forecast

## Which to shortlist

- **Need human interpreters, or might** → Interprefy
- **Pure AI, enterprise procurement, certifications required** → Wordly
- **Many languages per session** → model Wordly first
- **European counterparty required** → Interprefy
- **Predictable annual volume** → either; compare rates
- **Irregular usage, short notice, small groups** → neither model fits well, and that is a different conversation
