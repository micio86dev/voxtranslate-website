Zoom's translation feature is frequently assumed to do more than it does, partly because Zoom's own marketing page and support documentation disagree with each other.

## What Zoom actually does

**Translated captions. Text on screen. No translated audio.**

Participants read the translation while hearing the original. For some meetings that is perfectly adequate. For others it is a different product from the one people thought they were buying.

Coverage is **34 languages plus 3 target-only**, and it requires **Business Plus or Enterprise**, or the **Translated Captions add-on**.

## Verify against your own plan, not the pricing page

There is a documented discrepancy between Zoom's marketing material — which cites 46 languages and describes the feature as included in any paid plan — and its support documentation, which states 34 plus 3 and lists the plan requirements above.

Buyers who assume "our Zoom plan includes it" frequently find they are on the wrong tier. Check the feature in your own admin console before planning a meeting around it.

## When captions are enough

More often than people expect:

- Participants **read** the language competently but struggle with spoken pace or accent
- The meeting is **information-dense** — captions can be re-read, audio cannot
- Participants are in **noisy or shared environments** where they are muted and reading anyway
- Some attendees have **hearing loss**, for whom captions are the better accommodation regardless

Captions also help native speakers, which is a real and underrated benefit.

## When captions are not enough

- **Fast, overlapping discussion.** Reading captions while trying to contribute is genuinely hard, and participants end up passive.
- **Negotiations**, where tone and hesitation carry meaning that text drops.
- **Anyone with low literacy in the target language** but good listening comprehension — a common pattern that captions actively disadvantage.
- **Hands-busy participants**, such as someone walking a factory floor with a phone.

## If you need translated audio on Zoom

Zoom's own feature will not provide it. The options are:

1. **Human interpreters** with Zoom's interpretation channels, where your plan supports them — separate audio channels the audience selects
2. **A separate translation tool** running alongside the call
3. **A different platform** for those specific meetings

## Practical notes for captions

- **Speak at a steady pace.** Captions degrade fastest with rapid speech and heavy overlap.
- **Say acronyms in full the first time.** They caption badly.
- **Put numbers and names in the chat as well.** Captions get these wrong most often and the consequences are highest.
- **Tell people captions are on and how to select their language** — in their language, not only in English.
- **Check what your plan retains.** If you need the translated transcript afterwards, confirm it is saved rather than assuming.
