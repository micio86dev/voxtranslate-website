We do not publish verified figures for Webex's current native translation capability, so this guide will not state any. Feature sets and language counts in this category change between releases, and a number quoted from a blog post is worth less than thirty seconds in your own admin console.

What does not change is the set of questions that determine whether a native feature fits.

## Check these four things in your own account

**1. Translated audio, or captions only?**

This is the biggest functional divide in the category, and platforms differ. Captions mean your participants read while hearing the original; translated audio means they listen in their own language. For a fast discussion or a negotiation, the difference is large.

**2. How many languages in one meeting?**

Some platforms translate one language pair per meeting. If your calls involve three languages, a per-meeting pair limit rules the feature out entirely, regardless of how many languages appear in the total supported list. Test with a real multi-language meeting rather than reading the language count.

**3. Are external participants supported?**

Guests without a licence in your organisation are frequently excluded from translation features. If your translated calls are mostly with clients, suppliers or candidates, verify this specifically — it is the limit most often discovered late.

**4. What does the recording keep?**

Ask whether the recording and transcript retain the translation, or only the original audio. For distributed teams the asynchronous audience is often larger than the live one, and several major platforms drop the translated track entirely.

## How to test it properly

Run one real meeting. Not a vendor demo, not a scripted walkthrough:

- **Real spontaneous speech**, with the hesitations and restarts that come with it — read speech and spoken speech stress these systems very differently
- **The actual number of languages** you need, at once
- **An external guest**, if your real calls have them
- **Over your normal meeting length**, since some features are time-capped
- **Then check the recording** and the transcript

Thirty minutes of testing answers more than any comparison table, including this one.

## If the native feature does not fit

The options are the same across every platform: a dedicated event translation vendor, an AI translation tool running alongside the call, or human interpreters with a platform that supports interpreter channels.

Which one depends on the same four answers above, plus how predictable your usage is. Event vendors price in prepaid hour blocks bought through a sales call, which suits regular forecastable volume and penalises irregular use.

## Whatever you end up using

- **Load a glossary** of product names, acronyms and domain terms
- **Brief speakers to pause at clause boundaries**
- **Put numbers, dates and names in the chat**
- **Agree a fallback** for a mid-meeting failure before you need it
