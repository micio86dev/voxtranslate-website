There are three categories here, and they fail in different ways. Knowing which failure you can live with is most of the decision.

## Native platform features

The tooling already in your conferencing licence.

| | What it does | Languages | What it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zoom | Translated captions only — **no audio** | 34 (+3 target-only) | Business Plus / Enterprise, or the Translated Captions add-on |
| Teams Interpreter | Voice-to-voice with voice simulation | **10** | **Microsoft 365 Copilot**, not Teams Premium. Excludes guests and anonymous users |
| Google Meet | Voice-to-voice, voice preserved | **5 pairs, all English-anchored** | Business / Enterprise with Gemini |

Three constraints that decide most cases:

- **Google Meet supports one language pair per meeting.** A session with Italian, Spanish and German attendees is not possible, regardless of budget. There is also a 90-minute cap and no translation in recordings or live streams.
- **Teams Interpreter does not work in webinars or town halls**, and external guests without an M365 licence are excluded. The prerequisite being Copilot rather than Teams Premium puts the real entry cost around three times what most buyers assume.
- **Zoom does not produce translated audio at all.** If your audience is listening rather than reading, this is not a partial solution — it is a different product.

**When this is the right answer:** one language pair, English on one side, everyone licensed, under 90 minutes, recording does not need the translation. That is a real and common situation. Use what you have.

## Event platforms

Wordly, Interprefy, KUDO, Boostlingo. Built for conferences and congresses — one speaker, many listeners — and they do that properly.

The shared characteristics matter more than the feature differences: **none of them publishes a price**, all require a sales call, and all sell prepaid hour blocks that expire. Wordly's tiers start at a 10-hour minimum with 12-month expiry. Interprefy's unused hours do not roll over, and its pricing scales with the **number of languages** you need.

Wordly is pure AI, covers 60 speaker languages and 7 attendee languages across 3,000+ pairs, and carries SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and HIPAA. KUDO and Boostlingo lean on human interpreters — KUDO has a marketplace of 12,000.

**When this is the right answer:** large audiences, a procurement process that expects a contract, regular predictable volume, or a compliance questionnaire you have to pass. If you run four conferences a year and can forecast the hours, prepaid blocks are not a trap — they are just how that market prices.

## AI-native tools

Newer, priced publicly, sold without a call. Palabra publishes $0.04/minute for its speech-to-speech API and runs meeting plans from $45/month annual. JotMe sits at the consumer end, around $10-15/month effective, working as a bot that joins the call — which adds latency and does not work in native WebRTC.

**When this is the right answer:** irregular usage, sessions announced days rather than months ahead, or a budget that does not survive a procurement cycle.

## The questions that actually decide it

1. **Do people need to hear it, or read it?** If hearing, Zoom is out.
2. **How many languages at once?** More than one pair rules out Google Meet.
3. **Are there external participants?** Teams Interpreter excludes unlicensed guests.
4. **Does the recording need the translation?** Teams and Meet both drop it.
5. **Is your usage regular enough to forecast?** If not, prepaid hours that expire will cost you more than the sticker.
6. **Will procurement ask for SOC 2?** If yes, that narrows the field faster than any feature.

Answer those six and usually only one category survives.
