Qualification differs from audit in that you are deciding whether to proceed at all, usually with incomplete information and several candidates. Language barriers distort that comparison in a specific way: **you will rate the supplier with the best English speaker highest**, and that correlates with nothing you care about.

## Neutralise the English-fluency bias

The practical countermeasures:

- **Score against documents and evidence**, not against how well the call went
- **Use the same translated question set** for every candidate, so the variable is the supplier and not your improvisation
- **Ask to speak to the operational people**, not only the commercial contact — and note which suppliers make that easy
- **Weight written responses** at least as heavily as the call

A supplier whose sales lead speaks fluent English and whose production manager cannot be reached is telling you something. So is one whose English is poor and who put four operational people on the call with documents ready.

## Send criteria in advance, translated

Qualification questions should arrive translated, before the call, with a list of what to have ready. This is not a courtesy — it determines whether the call produces evidence or a scramble.

Include:

- Capacity and current utilisation
- Certifications, with expiry dates
- Named references in your industry
- Sub-supplier disclosure
- Escalation path with names and roles

## During the call

**Ask for specifics, not capabilities.** "How many units of this class did you ship last quarter?" is verifiable. "Can you handle our volume?" is not.

**Get numbers in writing during the call.** Say them, type them, confirm them.

**Ask what would have to go wrong.** A supplier who cannot describe their own failure modes has not thought about them, and this question survives translation better than most.

**Note who answers.** If every technical question routes back to the commercial contact, that is your finding.

## After the call

Send your understanding back in both languages within a day, with a deadline for corrections. Suppliers correct written misunderstandings readily and rarely volunteer them otherwise.

Score in writing while it is fresh, separating **what they demonstrated** from **how the call felt**. The gap between those two columns is where language bias hides.

## Platform note

Qualification calls involve external participants who may have no corporate conferencing licence at all.

**Teams Interpreter excludes external guests and anonymous users**, and requires **Microsoft 365 Copilot** rather than Teams Premium. **Google Meet** handles one English-anchored language pair per meeting — a problem if you want your own quality lead and their production manager in different languages. **Zoom** provides translated captions but no translated audio.

If you are qualifying suppliers in several countries and want a consistent process across all of them, the tooling needs to work for external, unlicensed participants in more than one language pair. That is the requirement to check first.
