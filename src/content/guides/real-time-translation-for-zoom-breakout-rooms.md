Breakout rooms break most translation arrangements, for a structural reason: translation capacity is finite and rooms multiply.

## The two problems

**Feature continuity.** What is enabled in the main session does not necessarily follow participants into a breakout. Verify this in your own account with a test session before an event depends on it — behaviour varies by plan and changes between releases.

**Human capacity.** An interpreter can be in one room. Six breakout rooms in three languages needs a number of interpreters that nobody budgets for.

## Design the rooms around language

Usually better than trying to translate every room, and the choice comes down to what the exercise is for.

**Group by language.** Each room works in one shared language, no translation needed inside it. Discussion is fast and natural. The cost is at debrief, where each room reports back and someone has to bridge.

Right when the value is in the small-group conversation.

**Mix languages and translate the debrief only.** Rooms are diverse, discussion inside them is slower and thinner, but the plenary is coherent.

Right when the value is in cross-group synthesis, or when mixing is the point — a diversity workshop that sorts people by language has defeated itself.

**Mix and provide translation inside the rooms.** Only feasible with AI translation, since it does not have the capacity ceiling interpreters do. Verify it actually works in breakouts on your plan first.

## Whichever you choose

- **Give each room a written task, translated.** If the framing is ambiguous, a group in their second language will spend the exercise reconstructing the question rather than answering it.
- **Give a written time signal.** A spoken warning broadcast into rooms arrives untranslated.
- **Ask for written output.** Groups report back in writing, in any language, translated for the plenary. This is far more reliable than a verbal report from a nominated speaker who may be the most confident rather than the most representative.
- **Brief facilitators** on pace and turn-taking if you have them.

## Test it properly

Run a rehearsal with real participants, in real breakouts, with translation running. Not a demo — an actual session with several people talking at once.

Breakout behaviour is where documentation and reality most often diverge, and the failure is discovered live in front of an audience if you do not check.

## A note on Zoom specifically

Zoom's native translation is **captions only, with no translated audio**, across 34 languages plus 3 target-only, on Business Plus, Enterprise, or the add-on.

In a breakout — a small group in fast conversation — reading captions while trying to participate is considerably harder than in a main session where most people are listening. If breakouts are central to your format, that is worth weighing before choosing the platform.
