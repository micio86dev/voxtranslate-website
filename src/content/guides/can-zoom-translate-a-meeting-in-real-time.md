The short answer is yes for text and no for speech, and the distinction decides whether Zoom solves your problem.

## What Zoom does

**Translated captions, in real time.** Text on screen in the viewer's chosen language, covering **34 languages plus 3 target-only**, on **Business Plus or Enterprise**, or with the **Translated Captions add-on**.

Each participant selects their own caption language.

## What it does not do

**Translated audio.** Nobody hears the meeting in their own language. There is no interpreter channel produced by this feature and no setting that enables one.

If your participants are listening rather than reading — which for most meetings is the default — this is not a partial solution but a different one.

## Verify against your own plan

Zoom's marketing material and its [support documentation](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0059081) do not agree: the marketing page cites 46 languages and describes the feature as included in any paid plan, while the support article states 34 plus 3 and lists the plan requirements above.

Buyers who assume "our Zoom plan includes it" often find they are on the wrong tier. Check the admin console before planning a meeting around it.

## When captions are genuinely enough

More often than people expect:

- Participants **read** the language competently but struggle with spoken pace or accent
- The content is **information-dense** — captions can be re-read, audio cannot
- Some attendees have **hearing loss**, for whom captions are the better accommodation anyway
- Participants are muted in noisy environments and reading regardless

## When they are not

- **Fast, overlapping discussion**, where reading and contributing at once leaves people passive
- **Negotiation**, where tone carries meaning that text drops
- **Anyone who listens better than they read** in the target language — a common pattern captions actively disadvantage
- **Hands-busy participants**, such as someone walking a factory floor

## If you need translated audio

Your options are human interpreters on Zoom's interpretation channels where supported, a separate tool alongside the call, or a different platform.

The native alternatives have sharp limits of their own: **Google Meet** does voice-to-voice but one English-anchored pair per meeting, 90-minute cap, and nothing in recordings. **Teams Interpreter** does 10 languages, requires **Microsoft 365 Copilot** rather than Teams Premium, excludes external guests, and does not work in ad hoc meetings or webinars.

There is no native option that handles three languages with translated audio in one meeting. Worth knowing before you start comparing.


## Can I translate it just for myself?

Yes, and it is a materially easier problem than translating the meeting for everyone.

[VoxTranslate for Chrome](/live-translation/for-zoom-web/) translates the audio of a Zoom
meeting joined **in the browser**, giving you subtitles over the call and, on tiers that
produce audio, a spoken translation with the original on its own volume control. It needs
no plan change from the host, appears nowhere in the participant list, and can speak the
translation rather than only writing it — which Zoom's own captions cannot do.

Two limits. It only works if you can join from the browser rather than the desktop app,
and it is one-directional: it translates what you hear, not what you say.
